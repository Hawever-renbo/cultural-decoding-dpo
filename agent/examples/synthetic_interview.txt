Interviewer: When did you start taking phone payments at the stall?
Rosa: Last year, after the students kept asking. I didn't want it at first. Cash I can count at night, but the phone money, I only see a number. My daughter set up the code and printed it, so now it hangs next to the menu.
Interviewer: What changed your mind?
Rosa: Honestly, I was losing the young ones. They come with no coins, only the phone, and if you say cash only they just walk to the next stall. So it was not that I trusted it, it was that I could not afford to say no.
Interviewer: And how does it work on a busy night?
Rosa: When it is busy I cannot check every payment. They show me the screen, I nod, and I keep frying. Sometimes I think, did that one really pay? But what can I do, stop the queue? My husband says we lose a little, but we would lose more by being slow.
Interviewer: Tomas, you still take only cash?
Tomas: Mostly cash, yes. I have the code but I keep it in the drawer. The fee is small, they say, but small every day is not small. And the money sits in the app for two days before it reaches the bank, and I pay my supplier every morning in cash.
Interviewer: So the delay is the problem?
Tomas: The delay, and also the record. With cash, nobody knows what I sold. With the phone, everything is written somewhere. I am not hiding anything big, but I don't want the market office to count my good nights and raise the rent.
Interviewer: Do customers complain?
Tomas: Some do. A regular told me, "Uncle, you are the last one." I laughed, but it stayed with me. For regulars I take it out of the drawer, because they will come back anyway and I know their faces.
Interviewer: Rosa, do you worry about the record like Tomas does?
Rosa: I think about it, but less than him. For me the record is useful, because my daughter can see at home how the night went, and she tells me which dish to make more of. Only when the network drops, then everything stops and people look at me as if it were my fault.
Interviewer: What happens then?
Rosa: Then I say pay me tomorrow, to the ones I know. To strangers I say cash or wait. Most wait, some leave. It is trust, but only as far as I know your face.
